How it works

One loop, run as missions, stopped by gates, explained on demand.

Here is what actually happens between "add the brand" and "post is live" — and where a person is in the loop.

Step by step

The loop, with its twelve internal stages.

Seven steps you see; twelve stages underneath. Each one is a piece of work the specialists can do, and each records what it used and what it produced.

  1. Know Research → Understand

    The brain reads what the company has said about itself — site, brochures, brand kit — and what the market is doing. It separates verified facts from prose, and records where each fact came from.

    AI does the workYou confirm facts

  2. Think Strategize

    With facts and context in hand, it proposes what to do: themes, a calendar, a campaign angle. Each proposal carries its reasoning and the evidence it rests on.

    AI proposesYou choose

  3. Create Create → Approve

    Creatives, captions, blogs and emails are drafted in the brand voice with the real logo and palette. Every draft goes to the approvals queue with its reason, expected outcome and risk.

    AI draftsApproval required

  4. Launch Publish → Promote

    Approved items are scheduled and published to connected accounts through your own token. Promotion — paid amplification — is a separate approval with its own budget gate.

    Executed after approvalSpend gated

  5. Convert Engage → Capture leads

    Comments and messages arrive in one inbox. The brain drafts replies in the brand voice; a human reads and sends. Leads captured from those conversations are planned next.

    AI drafts repliesHuman sends

  6. Measure Measure

    Results are compared with the expected outcome that was written on the approval. That comparison is what makes the next step honest.

    Partial today

  7. Learn Learn → Improve

    Corrections you gave in review, and what the numbers said, are written into brand memory — rules, corrections, learnings — so the next loop starts from a better place.

    Memory liveAuto-learning in progress

Missions

Work is organised as missions, not as prompts.

A mission is a goal with a scope: "Fill October with twelve on-brand posts for the launch." The brain breaks it into stages, runs the ones it is allowed to run in the current mode, and pauses at every gate with a card explaining what it wants to do and why.

  • One mission, one visible track of progress
  • Each stage records inputs, outputs and cost
  • You can step in at any stage, in any mode
  • Regenerating a stage invalidates approvals downstream of it

Every approval carries five things

  • The recommendation
  • The evidence it rests on
  • The reason for doing it now
  • The expected outcome
  • The risk if it is wrong

Three ways to answer

  • Approve — it proceeds to the next stage
  • Request changes — with a note the brain remembers as a correction
  • Reject — it stops, and the reason is recorded

Who can approve

  • Owners and admins approve for their workspace
  • Clients see and review their own brand only
  • Every decision is recorded with who made it and when

The "Why?" interface

Ask why, and get evidence — not a sales pitch.

Anything the brain proposes can be questioned. The answer lists the sources it used, the confidence it has in each factual claim, and what it could not verify. If the evidence is thin, it says so plainly and suggests what to add.

  • Confidence is per claim, not per post
  • "Insufficient evidence" is a valid, expected answer
  • Sources are the company's own: pages, documents, listings, approved facts
